# WHY TREK TUNISIA

Tunisia packs an extraordinary trekking range into a compact, reliably-accessible country. Within a single expedition you can traverse the **Jebel Dahar** Berber highlands past fortified *ksour* and troglodyte villages, descend through the **mountain oases and canyons of Chebika, Tamerza and Midès** in the Jebel en Negueb, walk the shimmering edge of the **Chott el Jerid** salt lake, and then commit to a multi-day, camel-supported dune crossing of the **Grand Erg Oriental** near Douz, camping wild beneath the stars.

It is one of the most accessible and safe-feeling corners of North Africa for remote trekking: short approach transfers, warm Berber hospitality, dependable weather windows, and a cultural layering — Berber, Roman, Arab — that gives every walking day a story. For an operator whose travellers want long-distance, off-the-beaten-path adventure with real depth, Tunisia is a genuinely fresh fixed-departure proposition.

# A LOCAL PROJECT YOUR EXPEDITION CAN SUPPORT

We believe support should be **fair, direct and evidenced** — the group visits the project, meets the people behind it, and contributes transparently. Choose one to anchor your fixed departures:

- **Berber village water, solar & school project (Jebel Dahar).** Support a highland village with clean-water access, solar lighting or a village school. The trek routes through the community for a half-day visit, and a per-traveller contribution funds an agreed, documented improvement.
- **Oasis palmeraie & foggara restoration.** Help restore traditional *foggara* irrigation channels and replant date palms in the mountain oases — directly sustaining the landscapes your group walks through. Travellers can join a planting or clearing session.
- **Anti-desertification reforestation near the oases.** Each departure funds and physically plants native, drought-tolerant trees at the desert margin, with photo-documented progress shared back to your travellers after the trip.
- **Women's artisan cooperative (weaving & pottery).** A visit and fair, direct purchase or commission supporting a Berber women's cooperative — meaning and authentic encounter on the same afternoon.

We supply an MOU, costings and evidence of fund use, so the project is a publishable, accountable part of your itinerary rather than an add-on.

## Sahara & Jebel Dahar Expedition Trek — 8–9 days (fixed-departure)

### Overview

A genuine point-to-point trekking expedition across the Tunisian deep south: from the fortified ksour and troglodyte villages of the **Jebel Dahar** Berber highlands, down across the **Chott el Jerid** salt-lake edge, and into a multi-day, camel-supported traverse of the **Grand Erg Oriental** dunes near Douz, finishing with wild desert bivouacs beneath the stars. Camel and 4x4 caravans carry baggage and resupply water; you carry a daypack. One full day is dedicated to visiting and supporting your chosen local project.

### Key Information

| | |
|---|---|
| **Duration** | 8–9 days, point-to-point |
| **Trekking grade** | Moderate to challenging (some long dune and highland days) |
| **Daily walking** | 4–7 hours / 12–22 km per trekking day |
| **Terrain** | Berber highland tracks, ksour ridgelines, salt-lake edge, open erg dunes |
| **Support** | Camel support caravan + 4x4 baggage/water resupply, camp crew, satellite comms, evacuation protocol |
| **Accommodation** | Berber guesthouses + wild desert bivouac camps |
| **Catering** | Full board; varied Tunisian **vegan / plant-based** menus throughout |
| **Group** | Small group, fixed departures |

### Highlights

- Point-to-point traverse of the Jebel Dahar highlands and ksour country
- Multi-day camel-supported crossing of the Grand Erg Oriental dunes
- Wild bivouac camps and starlit desert nights
- A dedicated project-visit day with a Berber community
- ONTT-certified guides, full camel/4x4 support and leave-no-trace camping

### Day-by-Day Itinerary

**Day 1: Arrival in Djerba & transfer to the Jebel Dahar**
Arrival, welcome briefing and route/safety overview, then transfer inland to the Berber highlands. Short acclimatisation leg on foot (1–2 hours, easy tracks) to settle into the rhythm and meet your guides and camp crew.
![Djerba](images/djerba.jpeg)
*Accommodation:* Berber guesthouse · *Meals:* Dinner (vegan options)

**Day 2: Ksour ridgeline trek — Jebel Dahar**
First full trekking day along highland ridgelines linking fortified granaries and Berber villages (5–6 hours / 16 km, rocky tracks and gentle climbs). Sweeping views over the pre-desert plains.
![Ksar Hadada](images/ksar-hadada.jpg)
*Accommodation:* Berber guesthouse · *Meals:* Breakfast, trail lunch, dinner (vegan)

**Day 3: Troglodyte villages & escarpment descent**
Trek through troglodyte-village country and down the escarpment (6 hours / 18 km, mixed descent and undulating tracks). Camel caravan carries baggage; first wild bivouac at the foot of the hills.
![Troglodyte village](images/berber-village-2.jpg)
*Accommodation:* Wild bivouac camp · *Meals:* Breakfast, trail lunch, dinner (vegan)

**Day 4: Project-visit day — Berber community**
A slower day built around your chosen local project: a half-day visit to the Berber village water/solar/school project (or oasis foggara restoration), with time to meet residents, see the work and contribute. Short linking walk (2–3 hours) to the next camp.
![Berber village](images/berber-village-1.jpg)
*Accommodation:* Berber guesthouse · *Meals:* Breakfast, lunch, dinner (vegan)

**Day 5: Salt-lake edge — Chott el Jerid**
Transfer to the trailhead and trek along the shimmering margin of the Chott el Jerid salt lake (5 hours / 15 km, flat but exposed terrain). Surreal mirage country; 4x4 shadows the route with water resupply.
![Dar el Jeld](images/Dareljeld.jpg)
*Accommodation:* Wild bivouac camp · *Meals:* Breakfast, trail lunch, dinner (vegan)

**Day 6: Into the Grand Erg Oriental**
Begin the dune traverse from near Douz with the camel support caravan (5–6 hours / 14 km, soft sand, steady effort). Climb the first great dune lines and make camp deep in the erg.
![Desert camp setup](images/desert-camp-setup.jpg)
*Accommodation:* Wild bivouac camp · *Meals:* Breakfast, trail lunch, dinner (vegan)

**Day 7: Deep dune crossing**
The committing heart of the expedition — a long dune day across the open erg (6–7 hours / 20 km, soft sand and dune crests), fully camel-supported. Starlit camp and Berber music around the fire.
![Desert camp music](images/desert-camp-music.jpg)
*Accommodation:* Wild bivouac camp · *Meals:* Breakfast, trail lunch, dinner (vegan)

**Day 8: Final dunes & desert exit**
A final morning of dune trekking to the erg edge (4 hours / 12 km), where the 4x4 caravan meets the group. Celebratory last desert camp or transfer toward Djerba depending on dates.
![Insolite desert camp](images/dunesinsolites.webp)
*Accommodation:* Desert camp · *Meals:* Breakfast, trail lunch, dinner (vegan)

**Day 9: Transfer & departure**
Transfer to Djerba for departure (or optional rest/extension day). Debrief, group photos and shared project-impact summary.
![Djerbahood](images/djerbahood.jpg)
*Accommodation:* — · *Meals:* Breakfast

# RESPONSIBLE & LEAVE-NO-TRACE TREKKING

Responsible travel is built into how we walk, not bolted on afterwards:

- **Locally owned and fairly paid.** Around **95% of accommodation** and **90% of meals** are locally owned; Berber hosts, guides and porters are paid **fairly and directly**.
- **Genuinely good vegan catering.** Varied Tunisian plant-based dishes — not eight days of plain couscous and vegetables.
- **Leave no trace.** We pack out all waste from wild camps, use **reusable water systems** with planned resupply rather than single-use plastic, and tread lightly on fragile dune and oasis environments.
- **Carbon-aware.** We track **CO₂e per night** and are **eCollective-ready** for your reporting.
- **Animal welfare.** Camel support animals are **ABTA-aligned welfare** standard — well cared for, fairly loaded and rested.
- **Real local support.** Your chosen community/nature project is funded directly and transparently, with evidence shared back to travellers.
